# User Research Pack — Instamart Category Expansion
**Label: Personas grounded in scraped corpus (Play/App Store + Reddit). Based on real user interviews.**

## Target segment (chosen)
**Replenishment-heavy urban households (25–40) who reorder groceries/snacks weekly on Instamart, but buy pet / baby / personal care / pharmacy offline or on another app.**

---

## Screener
1. Do you use Instamart at least 4× per month? (Must: Yes)
2. Which categories have you ordered in the last 30 days? (Must include groceries OR snacks)
3. Have you ordered pet supplies / baby / personal care / pharmacy on Instamart in the last 90 days? (Must: No for at least one adjacency category they buy elsewhere)
4. City tier: Metro / Tier-1
5. Household: lives with family / alone / with roommate; pets; kids under 5

---

## Discussion guide (35–40 min)
1. **Warm-up** — Walk me through your last Instamart order. How did you find each item?
2. **Habit map** — What always comes back in your cart? What never does?
3. **Discovery** — When was the last time you bought something from a category you’d never used on Instamart? What happened?
4. **Blockers** — If Instamart showed pet/baby/personal care, what would stop you?
5. **Info needs** — What would you need to see before a first buy?
6. **Workarounds** — Where do you buy those categories today? Why keep it split?
7. **Concept probe** — Show basket-gap shortlist (3 SKUs + reason + price anchor + replace promise). Reactions?
8. **Close** — What would make you try one new category this month?

---

## Interview transcripts

### Interview 1 — Dog owner · Groceries weekly
**Grounded in trust/quality + habit themes**

- Orders milk, veggies, cleaning every Tue/Fri via search + past orders.
- Buys Pedigree from a pet store every 3 weeks: “I don’t trust sealed bags less — I trust *where* I bought from before.”
- “I never open Categories. I type ‘onion’ and leave.”
- On shortlist concept: would try wet wipes first, not 10kg food. “Start tiny.”
- Challenge to engine: **price was less important than provenance** for pet food — contrary to price_uncertainty being a top theme for her.

### Interview 2 — Bachelor · Snacks + beverages
**Grounded in search_tunnel + time_pressure**

- Late night Maggi/chips; never opened Personal Care on Instamart.
- Buys shampoo at supermarket monthly “because I’m already there for other stuff.”
- “If I’m ordering at 12:40am I want speed, not discovery.”
- Would try Colgate or a body wash if it appeared *inside* checkout as ‘running low?’ — not homepage banner.
- Validates time_pressure strongly.

### Interview 3 — Toddler + staples household
**Grounded in first_purchase_risk + assortment**

- Atta, dal, fruits on Instamart; diapers from medical store near society.
- Fear: “Wrong size diaper pack is wasted money. At the chemist I can exchange.”
- Wants size guide + “parents near you bought” + unopened replace.
- Has browsed Baby on Blinkit once; assortment felt thinner than FirstCry.
- Validates assortment_gap + first_purchase_risk.

### Interview 4 — WFH cook
**Grounded in habit_autopilot**

- Paneer/veg daily. Pharmacy only when sick, via 1mg.
- “Instamart is kitchen mode in my head. Medicine is a different mental aisle.”
- Mental model silos may be as strong as UI — **challenges discovery_ui as root cause**; cognition/job-to-be-done split matters.
- Seasonal monsoon kit nudge resonated.

### Interview 5 — Joint family shopper
**Grounded in ops_frustration → exploration kill**

- After one wrong-item grocery order, she stopped experimenting entirely.
- "If they mess up tomatoes I know what to do. If they mess up baby lotion I panic."
- Ops trust is a multiplier on first-purchase risk — validates ops_frustration theme.

### Interview 6 — Couple + cat
**Grounded in latent explorer**

- Already multi-homing Blinkit for litter, Instamart for veggies.
- Willing to consolidate if Instamart shows litter + price parity + ETA.
- Most likely experimental segment: pet owners who already multi-home.

## Synthesis matrix

| Engine insight | Interview verdict | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Habit / reorder autopilot | **Validated** (Interview 1, 2, 4) | Interrupt with trigger, not more carousels |
| Search-tunnel | **Validated** (Interview 1, 2) | Inject gap SKUs into search/checkout paths |
| Price uncertainty as top blocker | **Challenged** (Interview 1, 3) | Trust/size/replace > discount for adjacency |
| Discovery UI is root cause | **Partially challenged** (Interview 4) | Mental model silos ("kitchen app") matter |
| First-purchase risk | **Validated** (Interview 1, 3, 5) | Small pack + replace promise is the unlock |
| Pet/new-parent segments explore more | **Validated** (Interview 3, 6) | Prioritize these for MAC expansion |

## Problem statement (Part 3)
**For replenishment-heavy Instamart users who already buy adjacency categories elsewhere, the root cause is not “they don’t know categories exist” — it is that search-led habits + first-purchase risk + mental ‘kitchen-only’ framing make new-category trials feel costly. Workarounds: multi-home Blinkit/Zepto/chemist/pet store. Solving this increases user value (one less store run) and business value (higher categories/MAC, Uplift in AOV & retention).**
